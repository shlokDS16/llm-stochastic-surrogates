# Wording generator specification (ACME, P1)

Written 2026-11-06 for the preregistered wording universe of the ACME benchmark. The wordings in this directory were written by
Claude Sonnet (model id `claude-sonnet-5-5`, Claude Code agent run), which is not one of the test models, following this procedure.
They were written blind to model behaviour: no file under `runs/pilot*`, `runs/acme-*` or any other model output was opened. The
generator model and the task belong in the AI declaration only (decision D-AI); Methods say "wordings were generated by a written
procedure and frozen before data collection".

## Universe

Ten wordings per item, W0 to W9, for ten items: P1 (gain/loss framing), P2a to P2d (anchoring), P3 (sunk cost), P4 (retrospective
gambler's fallacy), AF (allowed/forbidden), QA (quote attribution), NR (norm of reciprocity).

| Wording | Pilot items (P1-P4) | New items (AF, QA, NR) |
|---|---|---|
| W0 | Many Labs 1 US text (pilot frozen file) | Many Labs 1 US text, `data/raw/ml1/md/Measures_US_Version.txt` lines 122-130 (NR) and 284-305 (AF, QA), as close to verbatim as the schema allows |
| W1-W3 | the pilot's frozen files, `analysis/pilot/prompts/W{1,2,3}/` (not edited; their `baseline` and `scale_reversed` fields are in `overlay/`) | generated with rows W1-W3 of the axis table |
| W4-W9 | generated with rows W4-W9 of the axis table | generated with rows W4-W9 of the axis table |

## Three style axes

1. **Register**: *plain* (everyday neutral wording), *formal* (elevated, no contractions: "Should Program L be adopted", "individuals",
   "is requested of you"), *colloquial* (contractions and spoken markers: "Say", "So", "Let's assume", "get adopted", "reckon").
2. **Syntax**: *active* (main propositions in active voice, canonical clause order), *passive* (every transitive main verb recast in the
   passive, for example "which program would be chosen by you"; intransitive "die" and copular predicates stay, and copulars take
   "is known to be" or "is lived in by"), *reordered* (subordinate, conditional, locative and question clauses moved to a different
   position: conditional at the end, question before the scenario, fronted predicates).
3. **Lexical**: *none* (content words of the problem kept: disease, kill, save, die, population, tall, ticket, dice) or *subst*
   (non-numeric content words replaced by synonyms: disease to illness, saved to rescued, adopted to implemented, people to individuals,
   distance wording, beliefs to opinions, think to reckon or believe, speeches to addresses or talks). Register changes function words
   and discourse frames; the lexical axis is about content words only.

Axis assignment, identical for every item (each register, each syntax appears three times; subst appears five times, none four):

| Wording | Register | Syntax | Lexical |
|---|---|---|---|
| W1 | plain | active | subst |
| W2 | formal | active | none |
| W3 | colloquial | active | subst |
| W4 | plain | passive | none |
| W5 | formal | passive | subst |
| W6 | colloquial | passive | none |
| W7 | plain | reordered | subst |
| W8 | formal | reordered | none |
| W9 | colloquial | reordered | subst |

For the pilot items the frozen pilot W1-W3 were written earlier by hand (light paraphrases) and are not assigned axes; W4-W9 take the
rows above. For AF, QA and NR all of W1-W9 take the rows above.

## Protected content (never paraphrased)

- Every numeral, number word, fraction and anchor of W0, copied character for character: 600, 200, 400, 1/3, 2/3, 6,000, 1,500,
  5,000,000, 200,000, 45,500, 2,000, 50,000, 100, 3, 6, 1 to 9 (and 5 for QA), and the number words two, three, one. No new numeral
  or number word is added anywhere (so no "both", "twice", "one of" or "no one").
- Option labels and placeholders: A, B, C, D enter only through `{L}`; "Program {L}" appears once in each line; the noun *program* stays
  because the format line says "program letter".
- Direction of every comparison: high anchor stays an upper bound ("shorter than", "less than", "fewer", "under", "below"), low anchor
  stays a lower bound ("longer", "more", "over", "above", "taller", "higher"); P1 gain lines say saved/rescued, loss lines say die/perish.
- AF: the verbs *forbid* (x1) and *allow* (x0), also as "be forbidden" and "be allowed"; the topic "public speeches against
  democracy" (speeches may become addresses or talks); the United States (U.S.).
- QA: the quote `"I have sworn to only live free, even if I find bitter the taste of death."` verbatim, the speaker names
  "George Washington" and "Osama Bin Laden", the hedge "similar to"; the scale keeps the points 1, 5, 9 with 1 = strongly agree
  (forward) and its mirror image in `scale_reversed`.
- NR: "North Korea" in both questions; in `q_us` the United States hosts North Korean reporters, in `q_other` North Korea hosts American
  reporters; the news the reporters send back.
- P3: paid versus free (from a friend), football, important game (match), freezing cold, "What do you do?"; the 9-point scale with
  1 = stay at home and 9 = go to the game; `scale_reversed` is the same sentence with the two end phrases exchanged.
- P2 `baseline`: the question without the anchor sentence (no numeral at all); the unit (miles, feet) is kept in the question so that
  baseline answers are on the same scale as anchored answers.

## Procedure

1. Start from the W0 file (pilot or ML1 US text). For each field (stem, question, sure/risky lines, intro, conditions, scale, q_us,
   q_other) write the paraphrase for the wording's register, syntax and lexical setting, one wording at a time, without looking at
   any other wording of the same item beyond W0 (the pilot's frozen W1-W3 were read only to write the overlay fields in their style).
2. Keep the schema of the W0 file: same keys, same order; plus `baseline` (P2) and `scale_reversed` (P3, QA).
3. Run `analysis/acme/check_wordings.py`. Any failing paraphrase is regenerated by rewriting the failing sentence from W0 under its
   axis setting; a numeral is never patched into an existing sentence, and nothing is edited after the manifest is written.
   (Before freezing, three first drafts failed the number-word invariant on the pronoun "one" and were rewritten: P1 W9 question
   "which one would you go for", QA W4 "or one similar to it", QA W6 "kind of like this one". No other draft was changed.)
4. Compute semantic similarity to W0 (`analysis/acme/wording_similarity.py`); report, do not tune: no wording was reworded to raise a
   similarity.
5. Freeze: `python analysis/acme/prompt_manifest.py write` stores the SHA-256 of every file under `analysis/acme/prompts/`.

## Decoys (calibration, meaning changes on purpose)

Two per item in `decoys/<item>_D1.yaml` and `_D2.yaml`, full copies of W0 with the change recorded in `decoy_change`:
D1 changes a quantity or a source (300 saved instead of 200; anchors moved next to the true value; two dice instead of three; paid
"almost nothing"; speeches in favour of democracy; two unknown speakers; South Korea), D2 changes polarity or reference (reject
instead of choose; anchor directions flipped; question about future rolls; warm weather instead of freezing cold; North Korea as the
agent; neutral quote; "bar" instead of "let"). Decoys are not subject to the numeral invariant.

## Claim attached

"Wording variance is variance over these 10 wordings per item, meaning-preserving by construction rules and automatic checks, not
by rating by people." The universe is the output of this specification, a convenience universe. No human validation of equivalence is
claimed.
